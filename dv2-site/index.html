Where Australia Works

Which industries employ the most Australians, how that is changing, and where the jobs are.

By Sebastian Hsien, October 2026

The basics

Australia is one very big country, which leaves a lot of different territories with different cultures. Due to this, the job market is extremely diverse and constantly evolving.

As of February 2026, the Australian job market seems to favour more service based industries, with the top three being Healthcare, Technical & Scientific services and Construction..

How jobs have changed

The job market within Australia is constantly growing, even during and past the covid period. Most of the growth seems to come from the latter end of the graph, which makes sense as the population and labour force has expanded and will continue to do so for coming years.

Comparing the slope chart and the bump chart, we can see that the service industry has domincated the workforce all the way from even the 1990s, with scientific and technical services' workforce gaining a surge between 1990 to 2015.

Where are the jobs?

The distribution of jobs across Australia does vary significantly depending on the job type itself, however the distribution of ALL jobs seems to be spread quite evenly across the country.

Technical and Scientific services are dominated by the Eastern capital cities (Melbourne, Sydney, Canberra), however other services are dominated by Western Australia. Although there are clearly states that are more reliant on certain industries, the overall distribution remains relatively balanced.

The proportional symbol paints a clearer picture, showing that although Western Australia does indeed dominate the other services industry by percentage, the number of workers total is still lower or on par with the working population in the other states. The lowest number of workers, however, seems to come from the Northern Territories, and South Australia not counting Adelaide.

The dot density map finally provides a deeper insight into the workforce, by showing what gender then dominates each industry. The biggest examples would be Construction and Healthcare, as construction seems to be made up of mostly male workers, while Healthcare is predominantly female workers.

Who are the workers?

Following up on the gender distribution from the dot density map, the dumbbell chart illustrates the changing representation of men and women across different industries over time. Men seem to dominate in a larger amount of industries.

What you do depends on where you work. Education is mostly professionals, and transport is mostly drivers and operators

Healthcare seems to be the youngest industry, with nearly a third of workers aged 15 to 19, while agriculture has the oldest workforce, with a spike of workers aged 65 and over.
